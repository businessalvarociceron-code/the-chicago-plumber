Toilet Repair in Chicago, IL

Running toilet, weak flush, leak, or loose seat repair across Chicago.

Call (847) 809-2341 Ask a question

A toilet that keeps running may start as a faint refill sound after flushing, then become a tank that never stops sending water into the bowl. Over a week, a worn flapper can let water pass every few minutes. Over a month, a fill valve that fails to close can keep cycling, and a small leak at the tank bolts can leave moisture under the toilet or along the bathroom floor.

A weak flush can also change from an occasional double flush into a bowl that rises too high, leaves paper behind, or will not clear at all. If the toilet rocks when someone sits down, the movement can disturb the wax ring beneath the bowl and allow water or sewer odor to reach the floor around the base.

We check toilets in Chicago, Cicero, Stickney, and Oak Park for the tank fault, bowl problem, or base leak behind the symptom. A slow refill or minor handle issue can usually be planned, but an overflowing bowl, water at the base, or a toilet that will not flush should not wait because the bathroom floor, wax seal, and surrounding room can be affected.

Running water and weak flush

When we hear “the toilet keeps running,” we look at the flapper, flush valve seat, chain, overflow tube, and fill valve inside the tank. Water that moves into the bowl after the tank has filled often points to a flapper or flush valve sealing issue. A tank that hisses, refills on its own, or sends water into the overflow tube can point instead to the fill valve or water-level adjustment.

“The toilet will not flush,” “the flush is weak,” “the bowl fills too high,” and “there is water around the bottom” are different calls with different checks. A weak flush may involve the handle, lift chain, tank water level, rim holes, or bowl passage, while a wet base can come from tank bolts, a supply connection, condensation, or the seal below the bowl. We sort out the actual failed part before recommending work, since symptoms that sound alike on the phone can involve separate repairs and different material needs.

Tank and bowl inspection

During an inspection, we shut off the water when needed, remove the tank lid, flush the fixture, and check the fill valve, flapper, chain, trip lever, overflow tube, tank bolts, supply line, and shutoff valve. We inspect the bowl and base for movement, cracks, water marks, and failed caulk or seal material. We also check the tank water level against the overflow tube or manufacturer mark; on many toilets, the water sits about 1 inch below the top of the overflow tube. If the fill behavior suggests a supply issue, we can check water pressure, which should generally remain below 80 psi in a residential plumbing system.

A straightforward tank repair, such as replacing a flapper, fill valve, handle, chain, or supply connector, can often be completed during the same visit and commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes once the fault is identified. Resetting a loose toilet, dealing with a seized shutoff, replacing a damaged flange, or sourcing a discontinued flush valve, tank lid, or matching bowl may need a separate visit. We explain which component is failing and whether the repair is limited to tank hardware or reaches the connection beneath the toilet.

Fill valve age and shutoff access

The age and type of the tank hardware affect the scope of a repair. A common flapper or fill valve in an accessible tank is different from an older flush valve that requires the tank to be removed, or a proprietary part that matches only one toilet model. A brittle rubber gasket, corroded tank bolt, or cracked overflow tube can also turn a single-part repair into work on several connected pieces.

Access to the shutoff valve and the condition of the toilet connection matter as well. A shutoff that closes normally gives us a clean way to isolate the fixture, while a seized valve, hidden stop, corroded supply line, or toilet set tightly against tile can add work around the repair. If a loose bowl exposes a damaged flange or piping below the floor, the material, access route, and any required building sign-off can change the work beyond a tank repair.

Tank model and bowl location

Before calling, check the toilet model number inside the tank, on the rear tank wall, or beneath the tank lid if it is printed there. Let us know which bathroom holds the fixture, whether it is a one-piece or two-piece toilet, and roughly when the toilet or tank hardware was installed. Tell us whether water is running into the bowl now, whether the bowl is backing up, and whether water appears at the tank, supply line, or base. If the shutoff valve is behind a cabinet, boxed-in wall, or finished panel, mention that access before the visit. Call us at (847) 809-2341, especially if water is reaching the toilet base, because a failed seal can damage the floor beneath the bowl while a running valve continues sending water through the fixture.
