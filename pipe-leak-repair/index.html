Pipe Leak Repair in Chicago, IL

Pipe leak tracing and repair for walls, ceilings, valves, and water lines.

Call (847) 809-2341 Ask a question

People usually call us after finding “water on the floor,” “a wet spot in the ceiling,” or “a drip behind the wall.” Those are customer descriptions, but they can point to a split water line, a loose compression fitting, a failed valve stem, or moisture traveling along a pipe before it reaches the drywall. We start with where the water appeared, which fixture was being used, and whether the water meter moves when every faucet is off.

A pipe leak does not always arrive as a visible stream. A brown ceiling mark below a bathroom, peeling paint beside a kitchen cabinet, a warm patch on a slab, or a musty odor in a utility room can all come from water escaping at a joint. We check the pipe route, nearby shutoffs, fittings, and the surrounding wall or ceiling material rather than assuming the closest fixture is the source.

An active leak should not wait because water can soak framing, insulation, drywall, flooring, and electrical areas while the visible stain stays small. A dry mark from an old event may allow for investigation before repairs, but water that is running, spreading, or making the meter turn needs to be located and stopped.

Water on the floor

“Water on the floor” can mean a supply tube under a sink has loosened, a copper elbow inside a wall has developed a pinhole, or a pipe above the ceiling has opened at a joint. The location of the puddle matters: water at the base of a cabinet often follows the cabinet frame from a higher fitting, while water below a ceiling light may have traveled along a joist before dropping through drywall.

Customers also report “a wet ceiling,” “a hissing sound,” “the wall feels damp,” or “the water bill jumped.” A faint hiss can come from pressurized water escaping through a small opening. A damp wall can be a hidden pipe leak, but it can also be condensation or water entering from another part of the building. A meter that continues to register use with fixtures off is another useful clue when the property has a dedicated water meter.

Rust-colored water around a valve, green staining on copper, white mineral residue at a threaded fitting, and a recurring damp patch are different signs with different likely sources. We do not treat every stain or puddle as the same repair: the pipe material, failed connection, wall access, and amount of water damage can change the work and the cost even when the symptom looks nearly identical from the room.

Leak inspection and repair

Our leak inspection starts at the visible water, then follows the likely pipe route through the room, cabinet, ceiling, basement, or utility area. We inspect exposed copper, PEX, galvanized steel, valves, supply lines, joints, and nearby fixtures. We may isolate a branch with a shutoff, check the water meter, and use a pressure gauge to see whether the system loses pressure after water use is stopped. Residential water pressure is commonly kept within a range of about 40 to 80 psi, although the reading alone does not identify the exact leak location.

An initial inspection and isolation check often takes 30 to 60 minutes when the pipe and shutoff are accessible. A visible fitting, valve, supply line, or short exposed section of pipe can often be repaired on the same visit by shutting off the water, removing the failed part, fitting compatible pipe or connectors, and testing the joint under pressure. We protect the work area and confirm that the repair remains dry before restoring the water.

A concealed pipe may require a limited wall or ceiling opening to reach the damaged section. If the leak sits behind tile, inside a finished chase, beneath a slab, or near wiring, we explain the access needed before opening material. Larger pipe replacement, specialty fittings, extensive wall restoration, or work that requires coordination with building management may be scheduled separately after the source and route are confirmed.

Pipe access and shutoffs

The repair scope changes with the pipe material and the age of the connection. A corroded galvanized section can require more replacement than a newer copper or PEX fitting, because disturbing one threaded joint may expose another weakened section. A small leak at an exposed compression valve is usually more direct to reach than a pinhole in a line behind plaster, tile, cabinetry, or a finished ceiling.

Access is often the largest difference between two leaks that look similar from the floor. A pipe in an open basement ceiling or under a removable sink cabinet gives us room for tools, replacement pipe, and a pressure test. A pipe behind a tiled shower wall, inside a chase, or above a finished room may require careful opening and later restoration by the appropriate trade.

A working shutoff lets us isolate the affected line with less disruption. If a shutoff has seized, is leaking at the stem, or no longer closes fully, it may need repair or replacement before we can safely work on the pipe. Building shutoffs, shared water lines, and condominium utility rooms can also affect access and coordination.

Parts that match an older valve, uncommon pipe size, or specific fitting may need to be ordered rather than substituted. If the repair affects concealed building piping or a shared system, permits, inspections, or building sign-off may be needed depending on the property and the scope. We identify those conditions after looking at the pipe, not from a stain alone.

Fixture location and access

Before calling, note which room has the wet wall, ceiling mark, puddle, or hissing sound, and which fixture was running before it appeared. If you can see a valve, supply line, or pipe label, tell us the pipe material and any make or model printed on the valve body or fixture plate. Let us know whether water is actively running, whether the water meter is moving with fixtures off, and whether a local shutoff still closes. Mention if the pipe is under a sink, behind a cabinet, above a ceiling, in a basement, or inside a utility closet, since access affects the tools and fittings we bring.
