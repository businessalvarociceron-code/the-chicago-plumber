Drain Cleaning in Chicago, IL

We clear and inspect blocked drains across Chicago, Cicero, Stickney, and Oak Park.

Call (847) 809-2341 Ask a question

We hear about sinks that gurgle, tubs that hold dirty water, and floor drains that give off a sewer smell. We start at the fixture where the water is slow or backing up, then check whether the blockage is in the trap, the branch pipe, or a larger drain route farther along the line.

We check the sink bowl, shower tray, tub drain, and nearby fixtures because water can appear in the wrong place when two drains share a branch. Grease, soap residue, hair, paper, and food scraps can narrow a pipe gradually, while a solid obstruction can stop water suddenly. We also look for water around the cabinet base, floor drain, or cleanout cap before clearing a line.

We can usually schedule an isolated slow sink or tub drain before it becomes a complete stoppage, especially when no water is escaping from the fixture. We do not recommend waiting on a backup at a floor drain, shower, or lower fixture, because wastewater can overflow onto flooring and the blockage can affect additional drains on the same pipe.

Gurgling and sewer smell

We regularly hear customers describe a sink that drains slowly, a toilet that makes a bubbling sound when the shower runs, a shower tray filling around their feet, or a floor drain with a sewer smell. A kitchen sink may also back up when a dishwasher drains, while a laundry standpipe can spill water when the washer pumps out.

We treat a gurgle, standing water, and a sewer odor as different clues rather than one diagnosis. A gurgle can point to air moving past a partial blockage, while a recurring odor may come from a dry trap, residue inside the pipe, or a drain that is not moving water as it should. Water rising through a basement floor drain can indicate that the restriction is farther from the fixture than a simple sink trap.

We inspect the pipe route before deciding what needs clearing because similar symptoms can come from separate faults. A hair-packed shower drain, a grease-filled kitchen branch, and a blockage beyond a cleanout require different work and parts, even when each one starts with water draining slowly.

Drain inspection callout

During a drain inspection callout, we identify the affected fixture, run water where it is safe to do so, and check the trap, stopper, strainer, and accessible pipe connections. We ask which fixture was used when the problem began, then compare the water level and flow at nearby sinks, tubs, or floor drains. A sink branch is commonly 1 1/2 inches in diameter, while tub, shower, and laundry drains are often 2 inches, so the fixture and pipe size help us choose the right approach.

We inspect an accessible cleanout for evidence of a restriction farther along the route and assess the pipe material before using a cable or other clearing tool. Older cast-iron pipe can collect scale on its interior wall, while plastic pipe may have a different fitting layout and access point. If we open a trap, we inspect the slip nuts, washers, and trap arm, replacing a damaged washer or worn component when the connection cannot be reassembled properly.

A basic trap and fixture check can take roughly 15 to 30 minutes once the cabinet or drain opening is accessible. We can often clear an accessible branch-drain obstruction and test the fixture during the same visit; a camera inspection, difficult cleanout access, or work beyond the reachable branch may need a separate booking after we establish where the blockage sits.

Cleanout access and pipe material

The cleanout location, the pipe material, and the route from the fixture all affect the scope of drain work. A cleanout behind a finished wall, beneath stored boxes, or in a tight crawl space takes more preparation than one beside an open basement wall. We also need to account for a cleanout plug that has seized or a trap connection that cannot be opened without replacing a washer or fitting.

Older cast-iron pipe may have scale, rough joints, or corrosion that changes how we approach a blockage, while PVC and ABS fittings can have different access limits. Grease in a kitchen branch, hair in a shower trap, and debris at a floor drain do not clear the same way, and a recurring backup may call for inspection beyond the first accessible opening.

If the work shows a damaged pipe, a failed fitting, or a route that requires alteration rather than clearing, the job can involve ordered parts and, where required, building or municipal sign-off. We explain the condition of the pipe and the access issue before moving from a straightforward drain clearing to additional work.

Fixture and cleanout location

Before calling, we ask that you note which room and fixture is slow, blocked, or backing up, such as the kitchen sink, first-floor tub, basement floor drain, or laundry standpipe. We also need to know whether another sink, toilet, shower, or appliance causes water to rise at that fixture.

If the affected drain is at a kitchen sink with a garbage disposal, we can use the model number from the plate on the disposal housing to prepare for related access issues. We ask whether water is standing in the bowl or tray now, whether a cabinet, basement wall, or crawl space gives access to the pipe, and whether you can see a cleanout cap.

We can use those fixture and access details to send the right drain-clearing equipment for the route we expect to inspect. Call The Chicago Plumber at (847) 809-2341 before a backed-up floor drain or shower drain allows wastewater to spread across the floor.
